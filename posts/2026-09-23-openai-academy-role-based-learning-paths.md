## What changed

On September 21, 2026, OpenAI expanded OpenAI Academy, its free AI-skills training site, with four new role-based learning paths and 14 courses total:

- **Apply AI at Work** (3 courses) — for general knowledge workers. Covers writing clear instructions, giving useful context, reviewing AI output, building reusable workflows, delegating work to AI agents, and keeping a human review step in place.
- **Build with AI** (8 courses) — for developers using Codex or the OpenAI API. Covers planning and building software with AI, code review and quality, solution design, evaluating model output, building agents, retrieval (having a model look up information before answering), and running AI systems in production.
- **Lead AI Adoption** (1 course) — for managers and executives. Covers identifying business value, setting priorities, ownership, governance, and building a rollout roadmap.
- **Teach and Learn with AI** (2 courses) — for educators and students, focused on checking AI-assisted work against learning goals, source material, and assignment rules.

Each course ends with an assessment. Passing it earns an "OpenAI Academy course badge." OpenAI is explicit that this badge is a learning credential only — not a professional license, industry certification, safety certification, authorization to use AI in production, employment qualification, or proof that a learner's employer has deployed AI safely.

## Why it matters

This is aimed at closing the gap between "has used ChatGPT" and "knows how to use AI well for a specific job." Splitting the content by role — worker, developer, leader, educator — means the material is more directly relevant than a single generic AI course, and the assessment gives learners (and employers) a way to check that the material was actually absorbed, not just watched.

## How to use it

- Go to OpenAI Academy and pick the learning path that matches your role: Apply AI at Work, Build with AI, Lead AI Adoption, or Teach and Learn with AI.
- Complete a course's material, then take its assessment to earn the course badge.
- Remember the badge shows course completion, not a certification — don't treat it as proof of production-readiness or safety compliance on its own.
