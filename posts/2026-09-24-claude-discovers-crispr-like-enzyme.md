## What changed

Anthropic says its Claude agents discovered a new biological system inside bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria). The system is called an "array-associated reverse transcriptase," or ART for short. A reverse transcriptase is an enzyme that copies RNA into DNA. What makes this one interesting is that it sits next to a partner gene and rows of repeating DNA sequences, called arrays — a layout that looks a lot like CRISPR, the gene-editing system scientists already use to cut and edit DNA.

To find it, Anthropic ran about 950 Claude agents in parallel for 21 hours, using 210 million tokens (units of text an AI model reads or writes). The agents looked through more than 200,000 known reverse transcriptases in DNA databases, narrowed that down to 3,500 candidate systems, and then picked out 20 that looked genuinely new. One agent noticed the unusual repeat pattern next to one of these enzymes and flagged it as worth a closer look by human scientists. The enzyme itself was already known to exist, but no one had previously noticed this specific repeat-and-partner-gene pattern around it.

Anthropic set up a dedicated life sciences research group and lab earlier in 2026 specifically to run projects like this. Feng Zhang, a biologist at MIT known for his work on CRISPR, commented that this is "an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery."

## Why it matters

CRISPR-like systems are valuable because they can potentially be adapted into tools for editing genes precisely — a capability used in research and in developing some medical treatments. Right now, ARTs are just a discovery, not a working tool: nobody has shown yet that they can actually be used to edit genes the way CRISPR can. But the more important story here may be the method: a large number of AI agents searching huge scientific databases on their own, with very little step-by-step human direction, and successfully noticing a subtle pattern a person might have missed. That's a preview of how AI could speed up early-stage scientific discovery more broadly.

## How to use it

This is a research result, not a product. There's nothing to install or try. Anthropic says its life sciences group will keep publishing findings like this as it uses Claude for biological research.
