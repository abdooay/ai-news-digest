title: "OpenAI research: workers increasingly use ChatGPT for tasks outside their job"
date: 2026-09-19

---

## What changed

OpenAI published research, part of its "Work at the Frontier" series, analyzing more than 1.5 million work-related ChatGPT messages from about 6,200 workers tracked from April through July 2026. It looked at how often people used ChatGPT for tasks that are typically associated with a different job than their own — "cross-occupation" tasks.

The share of ChatGPT activity going to tasks outside someone's own occupation rose from 13.1% in April to 25.9% in July — roughly double. It's also sticky: workers who had already used a cross-occupation task returned to that kind of task again the following month 23.6% of the time, compared to only 8.4% for workers who hadn't used it before. On average, people returned to cross-occupation tasks 18.5% of the time the next month.

Some tasks were especially likely to be repeated: customer service conversations (54% return rate), advertising and promotional writing (44%), and marketing materials (37%). Financial explanations had a much lower return rate (15%). OpenAI also found that when people ask ChatGPT for help outside their own role, they tend to write shorter prompts and ask for fewer explanations or specific formats than when working within their own role.

## Why it matters

This suggests that people's actual day-to-day responsibilities may be expanding — a marketer picking up basic customer-service writing, for example — before their job title or role formally changes to reflect it. For employers, this points to a gap between what employees are actually doing with AI tools and what their job descriptions say, which is relevant to hiring, training, and how "skills" get defined going forward. It's a useful data point in the broader debate about how AI is reshaping jobs, based on real usage rather than surveys or speculation.

## How to use it

This is a research finding, not a product change — nothing to install or configure. It's a useful reference point if you're a manager trying to understand where AI tools are already expanding what your team does in practice, ahead of formal role changes.
