## What changed

Anthropic and Accenture announced a partnership to build a team of "embedded evaluators" — outside reviewers who work inside Anthropic, alongside its own teams, to evaluate and red-team (stress-test) its AI models. The work will be led by Faculty, Accenture's specialist AI unit, which Accenture describes as experienced in testing and evaluating models for several leading AI labs.

The evaluators' job covers red-teaming models, running alignment assessments (checking whether a model's behavior matches its intended goals and values), and testing model safeguards. According to Anthropic, they will have employee-level access: they can observe models during training, watch the decisions that shape development and deployment, talk directly with Anthropic staff, and report problems publicly.

Anthropic and Accenture each expect to invest at least $1 billion over the next five years in this evaluation work. The arrangement is non-exclusive — Anthropic says it's in talks with other evaluators, including the nonprofit METR, and Accenture says it will work with other AI developers in similar roles.

## Why it matters

AI companies normally evaluate their own models before release, which raises an obvious question: how do outsiders know those evaluations are honest and thorough? Embedding independent evaluators with real internal access — not just a one-time external audit — is a concrete step toward outside verification of how a frontier AI lab actually builds and checks its models. Anthropic frames this as following through on CEO Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier," which promised to bring in evaluators like this.

Anthropic is explicit that using outside evaluators doesn't shift responsibility away from itself: it says the models' safety remains its own responsibility, and that embedded evaluators are meant to make that responsibility "more verifiable," not to replace it.

## How to use it

This isn't a product or a tool — it's a governance and safety-process change at Anthropic. There's nothing for developers or users to install or configure. What to watch for: Anthropic says standards for evaluator access levels and public reporting are still being worked out, and it expects to announce additional evaluator partners in the coming weeks.
