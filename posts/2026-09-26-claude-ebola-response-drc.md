## What changed

Anthropic published a feature story about how Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant) is being used in the response to a Bundibugyo ebolavirus outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC). The outbreak was confirmed in May 2026, and the World Health Organization declared it a public health emergency of international concern on May 17, 2026. As of September 19, 2026, the outbreak had 7,672 confirmed cases and 3,699 deaths (a 48.2% fatality rate), spread across 7 provinces and 63 of 167 health zones. Treatment beds in North Kivu were at 94.7% occupancy.

Anthropic's Beneficial Deployments and Applied AI teams worked with the WHO's Regional Office for Africa (WHO AFRO), the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI), and DRC's National Institute of Biomedical Research (INRB) on several uses of Claude:

- **Situation reports ("sitreps")**: Claude extracts case and lab numbers from PowerPoint decks, flags trends, and summarizes district-level reports. This cut the time to compile a sitrep from a full day to under an hour, using Claude Skills (a way to package a repeatable task for Claude to run).
- **Disease modeling**: Claude runs multiple epidemiological forecasting models at once, helping logistics teams decide where to place treatment centers.
- **Vaccine tracking**: CEPI used Claude to build dashboards that organize research data across vaccine candidate proposals, making it easier for experts to compare options. Anthropic was clear that Claude does not make the scientific judgment calls itself — for example, deciding which blood samples to analyze for a vaccine's effectiveness against this specific virus strain remains a decision for human scientists.
- **Genome analysis**: Claude Science (an AI workbench for scientific data) assembles viral genomes from sequencing data and builds virus family trees using plain-language prompts instead of specialized bioinformatics commands.
- **Data cleaning**: Claude also helped clean and validate case-tracking data for a separate chikungunya outbreak.

Dr. Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director general of DRC's INRB, said: "I have fought this virus for fifty years. The tools have changed completely, but the rule has not: you beat Ebola by knowing where it is today, not where it was last week."

## Why it matters

Outbreak response depends on speed: knowing where cases are rising right now, not last week. Manually compiling reports from scattered PowerPoint decks and spreadsheets eats into the time responders have to act. Cutting that from a full day to under an hour is a real, measurable gain for teams working under pressure with limited staff. It's also a concrete example of AI being used as a support tool alongside expert judgment, not a replacement for it — the vaccine-tracking dashboards organize data, but scientists still decide what the data means.
