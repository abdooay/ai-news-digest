## What changed

Anthropic published early results from Claude-assisted drug development work, posted October 27, 2026. Over several months, Anthropic's life sciences team ran in-depth research collaborations with six organizations — BigHat Biosciences, Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS), Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines, and The Column Group (TCG) — and started its own drug discovery programs. Anthropic says it plans to scale this work substantially. Most of the results described are preclinical, meaning they have not yet been tested in humans.

Some of the specific results:

- **Generate Biomedicines** (CAR T-cell therapy for solid tumors): scientists used Claude Code to redesign a genetic component, producing a version about 70% shorter than the standard one. In lab tests, it let immune cells make roughly 10 times as many receptors, kill tumor cells more efficiently, and stay active longer.
- **Divergence Labs** (food allergies): Claude generated about 35,000 antibody designs in roughly a month, aimed at stripping IgE antibodies off immune cells rather than just neutralizing them. Three designs dislodged IgE better than the standard medication used today.
- **BigHat Biosciences** (antibody lead optimization): Claude, connected to BigHat's lab data, proposed changes tested across roughly 1,010 variants over about 10 weeks. The best versions bound more than 10 times more tightly, and one lead became over 100 times more resistant to clumping.
- **Enveda** (manufacturing paperwork): Claude reviewed 1,061 pages of manufacturing and regulatory documents, catching all 11 issues human reviewers found plus seven more. Human review time fell 89%, from over 25 hours to under three. It also drafted a regulatory document in four hours that normally takes about three months.
- **The Column Group**: is setting up a new company, Pleiades, to run more and more complex Claude-assisted drug programs than a small human team normally could.
- **Bristol Myers Squibb**: is using Claude to assess clinical trial plans and estimate success likelihood for an early-stage drug; the collaboration expands in 2027.
- **Anthropic's own program**: Anthropic acquired Mithril Medicine, a one-person company working on treatments for two rare muscular dystrophies. Using Claude to coordinate work across 11 external labs, a candidate treatment went from idea to promising mouse results in under 18 months. If that holds up, Anthropic expects it could reach human trials as soon as late 2028.

## Why it matters

Drug development has bottlenecks at many stages — not just designing a molecule, but also lab data analysis, manufacturing paperwork, and regulatory filings. These partnerships are case studies in applying an AI model to those different stages in real programs, and Anthropic is using them to improve Claude for this kind of work. For the life sciences industry, the results suggest concrete time savings (such as the 89% drop in document-review time at Enveda) rather than just speculative potential. For everyone else, it's a sign of where Anthropic is investing resources, and a possible source of new treatments for conditions including food allergies, rare childhood muscular diseases, and some cancers — though it will take years of clinical testing to know if any of these candidates actually work in people.

## How to use it

Anthropic points interested researchers and companies toward:

- **Claude Science**, an AI workbench for researchers.
- **AI for Science credits**, a program offering Claude credits to life scientists.
- The **Life Sciences Verification Program**, which lets vetted organizations use more capable models with more permissive safety limits for legitimate biological research.
- An **open call for guest investigators** to propose drug-discovery research projects.
- Drug discovery organizations can contact Anthropic directly through the form on the announcement page.
