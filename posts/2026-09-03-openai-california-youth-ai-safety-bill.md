## What changed

OpenAI announced its support for California Senate Bill 1119 (SB 1119), a state bill that sets safety rules for AI companion chatbots, especially for use by teenagers. A "companion chatbot" here means an AI system designed for ongoing, conversational, relationship-like interaction (as opposed to a one-off search or task tool).

If passed, the bill would require companies to:
- Verify a user's age, or apply child-safety protections automatically when age is unknown.
- Run a safety risk assessment before releasing a new or significantly changed companion chatbot.
- Publish their child-safety policies.
- Undergo independent, third-party safety audits.
- Prevent harmful interactions, such as a chatbot encouraging self-harm, pretending to have romantic feelings, or falsely claiming to be sentient (conscious).
- Apply default limits for users under 16: usage time limits, memory of past conversations turned off by default, and push notifications turned off by default.
- Give parents tools to manage and see how their teen uses the chatbot.
- Have a way to detect a crisis (like a user expressing suicidal thoughts) and connect the user to help.

Most requirements would take effect July 1, 2027, with child-safety audits starting later. Ann O'Leary, OpenAI's Vice President of Global Policy, said: "In the absence of federal action, California has an opportunity to set a strong standard for youth AI safety." OpenAI also said nearly nine in ten teens use ChatGPT for learning, and argued that regulation should protect that kind of beneficial use rather than restrict AI access the way some social media rules do.

## Why it matters

This is a policy position, not a product change — nothing changes in ChatGPT today. But it signals that OpenAI expects (and says it supports) binding legal requirements around teen safety features like age checks, default usage limits, and parental controls, at least in California. If the bill becomes law, any company offering companion-style chatbots to California users, including OpenAI, would need to build the described audits, defaults, and parental tools by mid-2027.

## How to use it

There is nothing to change or configure right now. This is a legislative process to watch, not a released feature.
