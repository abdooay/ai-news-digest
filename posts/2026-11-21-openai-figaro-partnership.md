## What changed

Le Groupe Figaro, the publisher of the French newspaper Le Figaro, and OpenAI announced a partnership to make Le Figaro's journalism easier to find inside ChatGPT.

Under the deal, ChatGPT can surface current and archived reporting from Le Figaro and its related publications — covering news, business, culture, and lifestyle — as summaries and excerpts with links back to the full articles on Le Figaro's own site. Le Figaro keeps full editorial control over its journalism; OpenAI doesn't get to edit or direct its reporting. Le Figaro, which says it has more than 600 journalists, also plans to roll out ChatGPT Enterprise (OpenAI's business version of ChatGPT) across several of its teams, and says it will explore using other OpenAI tools in its newsroom and reader-facing products.

The announcement does not disclose financial terms, how long the deal lasts, or technical details of how the content is licensed or delivered.

## Why it matters

For ChatGPT users, this means answers about current events may increasingly include excerpts from Le Figaro's reporting, with clear links to the original source, rather than ChatGPT working from older training data alone. For news publishers more broadly, it's another example — alongside deals OpenAI has made with other outlets — of a publisher choosing to license content and gain visibility inside ChatGPT rather than just hoping AI systems use it fairly on their own.

## How to use it

There's no setup required on the user side. If you ask ChatGPT about topics Le Figaro covers, you may see its reporting appear as a cited source with a link to the article.
