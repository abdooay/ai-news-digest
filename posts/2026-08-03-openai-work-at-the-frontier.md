## What changed

OpenAI published a report called "Work at the Frontier: How AI is Expanding What People Do at Work," the first in a planned series looking at how AI use is changing jobs. It's based on an analysis of more than 800,000 messages sent by U.S. ChatGPT users.

The headline finding: OpenAI calls it **task crossover** — work traditionally associated with one occupation showing up in the AI use of people in a different occupation. After excluding generic tasks (things like writing or summarizing that cut across almost every job), 43.5% of the remaining, occupation-specific messages were about tasks that belong to a different occupation than the user's own.

Some groups showed much higher crossover than others, once generic tasks are excluded:
- Customer experience workers: 77% of their occupation-specific messages were outside-occupation tasks.
- Designers: 75%.
- Human resources workers: 69%.
- Legal workers: 56%.
- Marketers: 53%.

The report also found that financial calculation and technology troubleshooting were among the top three "borrowed" outside tasks in all seven other occupation groups studied, and that marketing tasks travel especially widely across roles. Company size mattered too: among typical (non-heavy) users, workers in small teams (2–5 people) had crossover rates of 18.9%, compared with 16.3% for workers in organizations with over 100 people on the same plan — consistent with people in smaller teams more often picking up work themselves instead of handing it to a specialist.

## Why it matters

This is early, indirect evidence — based on what people ask ChatGPT to help with, not on official job descriptions — that AI is letting people take on tasks outside their formal role before that shows up in job titles or company org charts. OpenAI frames this as a leading indicator: usage patterns like this may show occupational change happening before it appears in standard labor statistics. For a business, it suggests that AI adoption doesn't just make existing roles faster — it can shift who on a team ends up doing which kind of work, especially in customer experience, design, HR, legal, and marketing roles, and especially in smaller organizations that lack in-house specialists for every task.

## How to use it

This is a research report, not a product change, so there is no setting or API to turn on. If you manage a team, the practical takeaway is to watch for where people are already informally picking up tasks outside their job description using AI tools, since that may be a sign a role or workflow needs to be formally updated rather than left as an ad hoc workaround.
