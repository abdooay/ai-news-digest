## What changed

As of November 3, 2026, text generated by **Claude Mythos 5**, **Claude Opus 4.7**, and **Claude Sonnet 4.6** carries Anthropic's text watermark on the platforms listed in Anthropic's "How Claude marks AI-generated content" article.

A text watermark is a hidden statistical pattern in the words a model chooses, used to identify text as AI-generated later, without changing how the text reads. Anthropic says the watermark doesn't change the meaning, quality, or readability of Claude's output, adds no extra tokens or hidden characters, and carries no information about you or your organization.

There's one exception: on Claude Sonnet 4.6, a request that sets `temperature`, `top_p`, or `top_k` (sampling settings that control how random or predictable the output is) to a non-default value might not produce watermarked text.

## Why it matters

This doesn't change anything about how you use the API — no new parameters, no price change, no effect on output quality. It mainly matters if you (or your organization) care about being able to verify that a piece of text came from Claude, for example to meet a provenance or disclosure requirement. The watermark gives a way to check that, for three more models than before.

If you specifically rely on the watermark being present, the sampling-settings exception on Sonnet 4.6 is the one thing to watch for — it's easy to trigger by accident if a framework or client library sets `temperature` or similar for you behind the scenes.

## How to use it

- No action is needed to get the watermark — it's applied automatically to supported models on supported platforms.
- If you rely on the watermark and use Claude Sonnet 4.6, omit `temperature`, `top_p`, and `top_k` from your requests entirely (including any a framework sets for you by default), since any non-default value on these may suppress the watermark.
- See Anthropic's "How Claude marks AI-generated content" article for the full list of models and platforms currently covered.
