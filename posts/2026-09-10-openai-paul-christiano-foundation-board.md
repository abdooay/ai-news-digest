## What changed

OpenAI announced on September 9, 2026, that Paul Christiano has joined the OpenAI Foundation Board. The OpenAI Foundation is a separate nonprofit that controls, and holds a large equity stake in, OpenAI Group PBC (the company that runs ChatGPT and the API). Christiano will be a non-voting observer on the OpenAI Group PBC board, and he will also join the Foundation's Safety and Security Committee, which oversees safety and security practices across OpenAI.

Christiano previously led alignment research at OpenAI from 2017 to 2021, where he contributed to reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) — a technique used to train AI models to follow instructions and behave the way people want. He later founded the Alignment Research Center, a nonprofit focused on aligning advanced AI systems with human interests. Most recently, he has worked as a Senior Tech Advisor at the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI), a unit of the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), where he evaluated frontier AI models, including risks with national security implications.

## Why it matters

OpenAI describes Christiano as someone who has been "an independent voice on whether the industry's safeguards are adequate," and says it wants that kind of scrutiny inside its own governance. For anyone tracking how AI companies are structured to manage safety risk, this is a concrete step: a researcher known for taking catastrophic-risk scenarios seriously now sits on the committee overseeing OpenAI's safety and security practices, rather than just commenting on them from outside.

This is a governance and personnel change, not a product change — it doesn't affect any API, model, or ChatGPT feature directly.

## How to use it

This is not a feature or tool — there's nothing to configure or turn on. It's worth knowing about if you're evaluating OpenAI's safety governance or tracking who oversees decisions about frontier model deployment.
