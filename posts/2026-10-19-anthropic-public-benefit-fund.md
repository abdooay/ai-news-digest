## What changed

On October 18, 2026, Anthropic announced the **Anthropic Public Benefit Fund**, a new entity that will hold a stake in the company and use it for public-benefit work.

Key facts from the announcement:

- Anthropic is committing **5% of its shares** to the Fund at the start. This is **subject to stockholder approval** — it hasn't happened yet.
- The stake can grow: the board can add another **2% for every 50% increase** in the company's value above a baseline it hasn't disclosed publicly.
- The Fund is a **wholly-owned LLC** (a type of company), not a registered charity (a "501(c)(3)" nonprofit). Anthropic says this gives it more flexibility in how it gives money away.
- The equity funding the Fund is money that would otherwise have gone to executives as escalating stock-based pay.
- Anthropic says the Fund's equity will **not** be used for lobbying or political donations, in the US or elsewhere.
- Combined with the 80% of equity Anthropic's co-founders already pledged at the company's founding, and an existing employee equity-donation-match program (about half of employees take part), Anthropic says roughly **20% of its shares** are now set aside for public benefit, before counting any future growth in the Fund's stake.

Anthropic says it will share more details on the Fund's governance and the exact valuation milestones in the coming months, along with a public report on its progress.

## Why it matters

Anthropic is a "public benefit corporation" — a company legally allowed to balance profit with a stated public mission, not just shareholder returns. This announcement is Anthropic putting a concrete ownership stake behind that mission, separate from its day-to-day business. The money is meant to fund things like large grants to nonprofits or direct support to specific groups of people — for example, workers affected by job losses from AI. Anthropic points to its existing $200 million commitment to research on AI's effect on jobs as one example of the kind of work the Fund could expand.

For most developers and users of Claude, this doesn't change any product or feature. It's a corporate governance and philanthropy move. It matters mainly as a signal of how Anthropic plans to handle the wealth its growth creates, and as a data point if you're trying to understand Anthropic's structure and incentives.

## How to use it

There's no product or API action to take here — the announcement doesn't describe a way for the public to apply for or request funding from the Fund. Anthropic says governance details and a public benefit report are coming later.
