## What changed

OpenAI published two related announcements in the past few days.

**"The AI policy window is open. We need to act."** (September 9, 2026) is a policy statement laying out four things OpenAI says it will do:
1. Push Congress for **mandatory, capability-based national AI safety regulation** — rules whose strictness scales with how capable a model is, rather than one-size-fits-all rules.
2. Keep supporting state-level AI safety legislation until Congress acts, including newly endorsing four California bills: one on independent AI risk assessments (SB 813), one setting standards for AI auditors (AB 1405), one on protections for children and teens using companion chatbots (SB 1119), and one on safeguards against AI-enabled biological threats (AB 1864).
3. Work with other AI labs on voluntary, industry-wide safety standards, starting with monitoring for "misalignment" (when a model pursues a goal in a way that violates human intent, without needing any malicious intent).
4. Push for compatible international standards on measuring AI capabilities and risk.

The statement argues that any national framework should apply only to a small number of well-resourced labs building the most capable models — not startups or smaller developers — and should not become "open-weights policy by another name" (open-weight models are ones whose underlying parameters are published for anyone to download and run).

**"Expanding AI access and cyber defense for federal, state, local, and tribal governments"** (September 10, 2026) announced a new multi-year agreement between OpenAI for Government and the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA):
- The normal $15-per-user monthly ChatGPT Enterprise license fee drops to **$0** for federal, state, local, and tribal government organizations, with 50% off usage costs.
- Every verified government entity is approved for "Daybreak Blue" access — a version of OpenAI's models with safeguards tailored for authorized cybersecurity defense work, like vulnerability research and incident response — at 50% off standard commercial pricing.
- The agreement runs 27 months, from October 1, 2026 through December 31, 2028.
- OpenAI says more than one million government employees already have ChatGPT access under existing agreements; this expands eligibility to roughly 23 million U.S. public-sector workers.

## Why it matters

Together, these two announcements show OpenAI pushing on both ends of AI policy at once: publicly calling for government to write mandatory safety rules for AI companies, while also expanding how deeply its own products are embedded in government agencies (including for cybersecurity defense work). The policy statement is notable for explicitly asking for *mandatory* rather than voluntary regulation — a shift in position for an AI company — and for naming specific bills it now supports that it hadn't backed before, citing "the recent jump in capabilities we have seen" (a reference to GPT-6 Astra, which OpenAI has said is the first model it's classified at "Critical" cybersecurity capability level).

The government pricing announcement matters practically for U.S. public-sector IT and procurement teams: a $0 license fee is a significant cost reduction that removes much of the budget friction to adopting ChatGPT broadly across an agency. The cybersecurity access piece is also notable given that AI-assisted attacks are explicitly named as a rising risk in OpenAI's own policy statement — the same underlying capability upgrade is being framed as both a risk to guard against and a tool to give defenders first.

## How to use it

- The policy statement doesn't require any action from developers or users — it's a stated position, not a product change.
- U.S. federal, state, local, or tribal government agencies interested in the $0 licensing and discounted cyber-defense access should contact OpenAI's government team to register interest and check eligibility.
- OpenAI is holding a webinar, "OpenAI OneGov 2.0: What Government Leaders Need to Know," on Monday, September 14, 2026, for agencies wanting a practical walkthrough of the agreement.
