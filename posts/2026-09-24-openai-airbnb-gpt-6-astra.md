## What changed

Airbnb expanded access to GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI's model, covered in earlier digest posts) and other OpenAI models for its engineering and product teams, through a new agreement that includes both direct OpenAI API access and access via Amazon Bedrock (Amazon's cloud service for running AI models). This builds on Airbnb's earlier use of Codex (OpenAI's coding-focused tool) and older OpenAI models.

Airbnb's CTO, Ahmad Al-Dahle, said the company's development teams are shipping roughly 80% more features than a year ago. Airbnb also said that in testing GPT-6 Astra on strategic documents and other non-coding writing work, one internal user reached an acceptable result in 3 to 4 rounds of back-and-forth, compared to more than 20 rounds needed with a competing model.

Beyond software development, Airbnb says it uses OpenAI's models for search ranking, fraud prevention, support for guests and hosts, and processing insurance claims, as it builds out a wider platform covering homes, experiences, airport pickups, and car rentals.

## Why it matters

This is a customer case study rather than a new product feature, but the specific numbers — fewer review rounds needed on written, non-coding work, and a large jump in shipped features — are a concrete (if self-reported, company-supplied) example of what a large company says it's getting from adopting a newer model broadly across teams, not just in one product area.

## How to use it

There's nothing new here to install — this describes Airbnb's internal use of existing OpenAI products (the API and Amazon Bedrock access to GPT-6 Astra). If you're evaluating whether to expand a newer model's use to non-coding, writing-heavy tasks in your own organization, this is a real-world data point to weigh, alongside your own testing.
