## What changed

OpenAI launched a beta program called "Businesses in ChatGPT." It lets a business claim and manage its own listing inside ChatGPT — similar to how a business might claim its page on a map or review site. Eligible businesses can also turn on a "Business Agent": a separate, clearly labeled conversation (distinct from ChatGPT's own regular answers) that answers customer questions and helps people explore what the business sells.

The beta is available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the U.S. first. Businesses in other places where ChatGPT is available can still start the process by claiming their listing.

OpenAI says it does not share people's conversations, or the conversations people have with a Business Agent, back with the business.

## Why it matters

For businesses, this is a new, direct channel to reach people who ask ChatGPT about products or services — and a way to see real data on how often they're mentioned, how many people click through to their site, and what topics come up in conversations about them. For everyday ChatGPT users, it means some answers about businesses may now come from a business's own agent rather than ChatGPT's general knowledge — though OpenAI says these are kept clearly separate and labeled.

## How to use it

If you run an eligible business:

1. Go to OpenAI's Business Hub and claim your business listing.
2. Once verified, you can create a Business Agent, update your business details, and upload product feeds with current pricing and availability.
3. Check the dashboard for aggregated insights: how often your business is mentioned, how many website clicks you get, and what topics people commonly ask about.

If you're just a ChatGPT user, there's nothing to set up — you may simply start seeing business-provided answers appear as a separate, labeled conversation when you ask about certain companies.
