## What changed

On August 3, 2026, OpenAI published a blog post responding to a lawsuit Apple filed against it. Apple's lawsuit centers on two people: Chang Liu, a former Apple employee whose last day at Apple was January 22, 2026, and Tang Tan, a 24-year Apple veteran. Apple alleges the two improperly accessed and used Apple's confidential information.

Everything below is OpenAI's own account, published in its own defense — this post has not independently verified Apple's version of events, and Apple's court filings were not reviewed for this piece.

According to OpenAI's post:
- Apple's outside lawyers "emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names" when first trying to contact OpenAI about the dispute, and OpenAI received no follow-up for five months before Apple filed suit in May 2026.
- Apple claims a conversation happened with OpenAI's General Counsel that, OpenAI says, Apple now "concede[s] never happened."
- OpenAI describes messages showing Chang Liu and Apple employees exchanging files around the time of his departure, and says Apple employees later asked Liu for help locating certain information — which OpenAI characterizes as "a common issue with Apple" around access management when employees leave.
- OpenAI defends Tang Tan, saying he "has always been clear with the team that we do not want, and must not use, any confidential information from other companies."
- OpenAI's core position: "Apple's request for a preliminary injunction is both based on false information and completely unnecessary because we do not have, nor want, any of their trade secrets." OpenAI calls the lawsuit "careless, aggressive and oddly personal."

## Why it matters

This is a public, high-profile trade-secrets dispute between two major AI and hardware companies, playing out partly through dueling public statements rather than only in court. For anyone following the AI industry, it's worth knowing this dispute exists and that it involves specific named individuals and a preliminary injunction request — a court order Apple is seeking to stop OpenAI or the individuals from doing something before the case is fully resolved. It does not, on its own, change any OpenAI product, pricing, or API.
