## What changed

OpenAI Academy (OpenAI's education and training arm) and Older Adults Technology Services (OATS, part of AARP) launched the "Older Adults AI Skills Jam," a free, in-person program to teach older adults how to use ChatGPT confidently and safely. It runs across 10 communities in the U.S., including sites in Denver, Miami, San Antonio, Montgomery County, Queens, St. Louis, the Twin Cities, Nashville, Fresno, and Idaho, working with local partners like Senior Planet locations, public libraries, and community centers.

OpenAI says the share of ChatGPT messages from users aged 55 and older grew from about 6% to nearly 10% of U.S. usage over the past year, and that ChatGPT gets tens of millions of weekly questions related to evaluating whether something might be a scam.

The workshops cover practical uses — trip planning, understanding documents, exploring hobbies, staying in touch with family — alongside a specific focus on scam detection, teaching a "pause, think, and ask" approach and how to spot warning signs like urgent language, requests for secrecy, and suspicious links.

## Why it matters

Older adults are a fast-growing group of ChatGPT users but are also a common target for online scams, and many have had less exposure to AI tools than younger users. A structured, in-person, free program — rather than assuming people will just figure it out — lowers the barrier to safe use for a group that benefits from hands-on teaching. It's also a concrete example of an AI company partnering with an existing trusted organization (AARP/OATS) rather than building outreach from scratch.

## How to use it

- The workshops are in-person and location-specific — check the partner organizations listed above (Senior Planet, local libraries, senior centers) in the 10 named cities/regions for sign-up details.
- The core safety habit taught is "pause, think, and ask" before acting on anything urgent, secretive, or containing suspicious links, whether that message came from a person or was drafted with AI help.
