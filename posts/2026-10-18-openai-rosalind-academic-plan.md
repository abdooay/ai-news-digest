## What changed

OpenAI launched the **Rosalind Academic Plan**, a free way for university research labs to use its life-sciences AI tools. The plan includes:

- **GPT-Rosalind**, OpenAI's model built for life-sciences work such as drug and protein design.
- The **Rosalind Workbench**, which brings together specialized tools for areas like medicinal chemistry, genomics, and wet-lab assistance (planning and support for hands-on lab experiments).

Eligible labs at U.S. universities can get up to 30 seats, at the "Business Standard" subscription level, at no cost for up to 12 months. Seats also include access to all of OpenAI's commercially available frontier (most advanced) models, within the plan's usage limits. GPT-Rosalind itself needs separate "trusted-access" approval on top of that.

The plan does not include API access at this time.

## Why it matters

AI tools for scientific research, especially specialized ones like GPT-Rosalind, can be too expensive for academic labs running on grant budgets. Removing the cost for up to a year lowers that barrier, letting more university scientists try AI-assisted work on things like candidate drugs, therapeutic proteins, and basic research into disease (the announcement mentions neurodegeneration as an example).

OpenAI also says research data shared through this plan is not used to train its models by default, which matters for labs worried about leaking unpublished results.

## How to use it

- A principal investigator (PI) or lab lead applies directly if their university doesn't already have an OpenAI Enterprise agreement. If the university does have one, the lab applies through that university's existing Enterprise access process instead.
- The application describes the lab's research and intended use.
- OpenAI verifies the institution, the applicant's identity and authority to apply, and requires the lab to name an institutional safety contact.
- Once approved, the PI or lab lead manages which lab members (up to 30) get seats, and is responsible for how the plan is used. Monitoring and other safeguards continue after approval.
