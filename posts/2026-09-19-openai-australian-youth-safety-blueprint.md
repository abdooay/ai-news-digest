## What changed

OpenAI published the Australian Youth Safety Blueprint, a six-part roadmap describing how it wants to make AI safer for young people in Australia. The six parts cover: AI literacy (teaching people how to use AI well), age-appropriate safeguards, privacy-protective age verification (checking a user's age without collecting unnecessary personal data), connections to crisis support services, parental controls, and accountability measures for companies.

This follows OpenAI's rollout of ChatGPT for Teens in Australia in August 2026 — a default experience for users identified as being 13 to 17 years old, with extra safeguards built in.

OpenAI states its position plainly: "the responsibility for safety should not fall primarily on young people or their families. Companies must continue to build protections into their products from the outset."

## Why it matters

Governments and regulators, including in Australia, have been pushing tech companies for clearer commitments on protecting minors online. Publishing a specific blueprint — rather than general safety language — gives outside groups (parents, regulators, researchers) something concrete to hold OpenAI to. For Australian users specifically, it signals what protections to expect from ChatGPT for Teens and where OpenAI says it still needs outside input.

## How to use it

There's no setup required by users. OpenAI says it will keep working with teens, parents, educators, researchers, and policymakers to develop evidence-based safety standards. Parents of teen ChatGPT users in Australia should expect the age-appropriate safeguards and parental controls described in the blueprint to apply to the existing ChatGPT for Teens experience.
