## What changed

Anthropic expanded its AI for Science program, in an announcement published October 28, 2026. AI for Science gives free Claude credits to researchers working on high-impact scientific projects. It started in May 2025 and, until now, mainly supported individual scientists one at a time.

The expansion adds 20 partner foundations, institutions, and research groups worldwide, in three groups:

- **Science funders and societies** that will distribute credits to their own researchers: the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, the Simons Foundation, Schmidt Sciences, the Open Source for Science Fund (a program of Renaissance Philanthropy), the US National Science Foundation, and CAS (a division of the American Chemical Society).
- **Institutions** that will use credits internally: the Space Telescope Science Institute, the Wellcome Sanger Institute, the Francis Crick Institute, Imperial College London's Department of Life Sciences, EMBL, and the Gladstone Institutes.
- **Research projects** that receive credits directly: FlyWire (Princeton University, neuroscience), LEAP (Columbia University, Earth system science), AstroPath (Johns Hopkins University, cancer research), gnomAD (Broad Institute, genomics and rare disease, which pools DNA data from over 800,000 people), Tau Ceti (mathematics), and the Centre for Population Genomics (Australia).

Two partners are running deeper collaborations rather than just receiving credits: HHMI Janelia Research Campus co-developed the Model Hardware Standard, a shared specification that lets AI agents safely operate lab instruments (currently in research preview), and the Allen Institute is building teams of AI agents to analyze its large open datasets on the brain, cells, and immune system.

Anthropic also launched a discounted Team plan for scientists, and says the program has already supported hundreds of projects across biology, medicine, physics, chemistry, climate science, and mathematics. The announcement doesn't say how large the credit grants are.

## Why it matters

Scientific research often runs on tight, grant-funded budgets, so the cost of AI tools can be a real barrier to trying them. By routing credits through foundations and institutions instead of handing them out one researcher at a time, Anthropic can reach far more scientists with less individual paperwork — a funder like the National Science Foundation can pass credits on to many grantees at once. For researchers at the partner institutions, this means access to Claude is now a standing benefit of their affiliation, not something each person has to separately apply for.

## How to use it

- Researchers at one of the listed institutions, funders, or projects should check with their own organization (for example, their NSF grant program or their institution's research computing office) about how to claim AI for Science credits.
- Scientists not at a partner organization can apply individually; Anthropic's AI for Science page has the application details.
- Teams of scientists who want a lower-cost paid plan can look into the new discounted Team plan for scientists mentioned in the announcement.
