## What changed

Cooley, an international law firm, built a tool called GO Public on top of ChatGPT Work (OpenAI's ChatGPT product for business use) to speed up work on initial public offerings (IPOs). Cooley says it advised on 180 deals in 2025, worth over $51.5 billion, and has led the US issuer-side IPO market for a decade.

An IPO involves thousands of concurrent tasks and huge volumes of documents and information that need to be synthesized quickly. GO Public uses an "agentic" framework — meaning it can work through multi-step tasks somewhat independently — to analyze information automatically, but it's designed to require lawyer review and sign-off at defined checkpoints rather than working unsupervised. The output is IPO preparation material customized to each deal.

## Why it matters

This is a case study of a law firm building its own specialized tool on top of a general AI product, rather than waiting for an off-the-shelf legal AI product. It's an example of the pattern OpenAI has been highlighting for its Astra for Law push (covered in an earlier digest post) — professional firms extending a general-purpose model into workflow-specific tools, while keeping a human lawyer as the final check on judgment calls. For high-stakes, document-heavy legal work like IPOs, that human-in-the-loop design is likely a big part of why firms are comfortable adopting it.

## How to use it

GO Public is Cooley's own internal/client tool, not a public product — there's nothing external to sign up for. The relevant takeaway for other firms or businesses: OpenAI's ChatGPT Work product supports building custom agentic tools with checkpoints for human review, which is the specific pattern Cooley used here.
