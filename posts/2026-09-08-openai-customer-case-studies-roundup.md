## What changed

In the first week of September 2026, OpenAI published several customer case studies. These are OpenAI-selected examples, not independent reviews, but each includes specific, checkable numbers rather than vague claims.

**Legora (legal AI platform)** — [Legora reviewed 41 documents in minutes with GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/legora-financial-statement-review-with-astra), published September 3, 2026. Legora's agent used GPT-6 Astra to do "financial-statement tie-out" work — checking that numbers match up across draft accounts, trial balances, and prior-year accounts. On a test run with 41 documents and four deliberately planted errors (including a £500,000 gap in revenue notes), the agent found all four errors and completed about 50 more verification checks than Legora's previous model, while still catching everything the old model caught. Legora reported roughly a 40% improvement over its previous model specifically on this financial-statement task.

**Playco (game development)** — [Playco cut manual fixes 50% prototyping games with GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/playco-game-prototyping-with-astra), published September 3, 2026. Playco builds "Playbot," an AI coding tool for game developers that works inside engines like Unity and Godot. Using GPT-6 Astra, Playco says it needed 50% fewer manual fixes compared to its previous model, and built three playable game prototypes from one base project, with most prototypes working correctly on the first attempt.

**ATV Big Air Tour (small live-events business)** — [ATV Big Air Tour turned 3 days of work into 3 hours with ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/atv-big-air-tour), published September 2, 2026. This two-person touring show business used ChatGPT Work for three tasks: checking that event listings across roughly 30 websites are accurate (cut from about 8 hours/week to 1 hour/week), organizing merchandise inventory and reorders from photos (cut from 2-3 days to 2-3 hours), and auditing their website so AI search tools can find it more easily (they reported search-related hits to their site rose from 183 to 2,421 in a month, a roughly 13x increase).

**Gilbert + Tobin (Australian law firm)** — [How law firm Gilbert + Tobin governs and scales AI with OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gilbert-tobin), published September 1, 2026. The firm rolled out ChatGPT Enterprise across operations, marketing, recruitment, finance, and parts of legal practice, plus Codex for automating multi-step workflows. As of June 2026, 87% of enabled accounts were actively in use — more than double the firm's usual rate for adopting a new tool. Recruitment tasks that used to take about 4 hours now take about 20 minutes; Codex-based automation prepared audit reports for 300 entities (saving roughly a full day of work) and cut conflict/compliance checks from 8 hours to minutes.

## Why it matters

Individually, these are marketing case studies and should be read as such — they're picked and published by OpenAI to showcase its products. But taken together, they show a consistent pattern: businesses using GPT-6 Astra or Codex for well-defined, repetitive checking and drafting work (document review, compliance checks, inventory organization) are reporting large time reductions, often turning multi-hour or multi-day tasks into a task measured in minutes or hours. That's a useful pattern to know about if you're evaluating whether these tools fit a similar repetitive-work problem at your own organization.

## How to use it

If you have a workflow that looks like these examples — comparing documents against a source of truth, checking a website or listings for consistency, or running the same multi-step process across many records — these case studies suggest it's worth testing GPT-6 Astra (via ChatGPT or the API) or Codex on a similar task at small scale before committing to it broadly. Note that all four figures above come from the companies themselves, presented in OpenAI's own marketing materials, so treat them as a starting point for your own testing rather than a guarantee.
