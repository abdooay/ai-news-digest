## What changed

Anthropic opened an application for life-sciences researchers to become "guest investigators." Selected research groups work directly with Anthropic researchers for 8 to 12 weeks on a proposed project, using Claude at no cost, to produce analyses, artifacts, or tools useful for their own research.

Who can apply: principal investigators and their research groups at academic institutions, nonprofits, or government labs. The focus areas named are causal human biology, structural and computational molecular biology, medicinal chemistry, drug discovery, and clinical science.

To apply, researchers submit a project proposal through anthropic.com/guest-investigator-program, explaining how working directly with Anthropic researchers would help their project and making a reasonable case they can produce some output — such as preliminary findings, answers to open questions, open datasets, or public resources — within the collaboration window. Applications should only include publicly available information; Anthropic says it is not asking for data.

Results get published jointly, with the data and code open-sourced. Guest investigators keep rights to their own data and to any follow-on research. Some projects may run longer than the initial 8-to-12-week window.

The application window is open for about two weeks from the October 22, 2026 announcement, so it closes in early November 2026 (no exact date given). Anthropic says it will email selected applicants about two weeks after applications close.

## Why it matters

This gives outside biology and chemistry researchers free, hands-on access to Anthropic's models and research staff, rather than just API credits. It's aimed at researchers who have a scientific question they think Claude could help answer quickly, not at those needing months of open-ended exploration — Anthropic is explicit that it doesn't expect most open problems to be fully solved in the window, just moved forward with preliminary evidence.

It's one of several related Anthropic programs for scientists: the Life Sciences Verification Program (direct access to Anthropic's most capable public models for biology) and the AI for Science initiative (Claude credits for researchers).

## How to use it

- If you lead a life-sciences research group at an academic institution, nonprofit, or government lab, prepare a proposal and submit it at anthropic.com/guest-investigator-program.
- Keep the proposal to publicly available information — don't include private or proprietary data.
- Explain concretely what you could produce (preliminary results, an open dataset, a public tool) within 8 to 12 weeks of direct collaboration.
- Apply soon — the window is about two weeks from October 22, 2026, and no exact closing date was published.
