## What changed

OpenAI introduced Astra for Law, a version of its GPT-6 Astra model built specifically for legal work. It combines the base model with a dedicated legal search index — described as covering more than 230 million URLs of U.S. case law, statutes, regulations, and court rules — plus custom instructions tuned for legal analysis and writing.

On OpenAI's own legal research benchmark, Astra for Law scored 54.0% correctness, compared to 38.7% for plain GPT-6 Astra — about a 40% improvement. OpenAI also reports it pulls in 24% more reference cases and up to 54% more relevant passages from court opinions than the base model.

Beyond search, it's meant to handle full legal workflows: case analysis, building arguments, contract review, and flagging risk, using instructions tailored for legal reasoning rather than general-purpose writing.

It comes with a Zero Data Retention option for API customers, 26 plugins connecting to legal tools like Relativity, Clio, and Thomson Reuters, and integration with ChatGPT Enterprise and Word. OpenAI named legal-tech companies Harvey and Legora as API customers, and law firms Sullivan & Cromwell, Ropes & Gray, and Cooley as examples of firms building custom implementations.

## Why it matters

General-purpose AI models are often weak at legal research specifically, because case law and statutes need precise sourcing and up-to-date coverage that a generic web index doesn't reliably provide. A 40% jump in correctness on OpenAI's own legal benchmark, plus a purpose-built case-law index, is aimed directly at that gap. For law firms and legal-tech vendors already building on OpenAI's models, this is a more specialized (and reportedly more accurate) foundation than using GPT-6 Astra directly.

## How to use it

- Access is currently rolling out as "Trusted Access" inside ChatGPT and Codex, for selected law firms.
- API access is described as "coming soon" — not yet generally available.
- Interested firms or legal-tech companies should contact OpenAI for early access.
- No pricing was disclosed in the announcement.
