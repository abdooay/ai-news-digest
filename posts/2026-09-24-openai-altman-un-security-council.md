## What changed

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman addressed the United Nations Security Council on AI. He framed AI's likely future as a choice between two outcomes: a "Renaissance of creativity and discovery" or an "Industrial Revolution of upheaval and disarray." He said AI should expand what people can do themselves, rather than replace human judgment or concentrate power in a few hands.

Altman named two risks he called unacceptable: AI systems advancing faster than institutions can monitor or regulate them, and powerful AI ending up controlled by too few actors able to impose their view on everyone else. He said companies should not build systems they cannot keep under human control.

He called for international cooperation on four things: shared standards for measuring AI capabilities and assessing risk, common frameworks that let countries verify each other's compliance, protocols for reporting AI-related incidents so lessons get shared globally, and secure government-to-government channels for sharing information about vulnerabilities and threats. He added that these standards should be designed so they don't lock in the current leading companies, and should leave room for a diverse set of AI developers.

## Why it matters

This is a policy speech, not a product announcement, but it signals what kind of AI governance OpenAI's leadership says it wants to see — international standards and verification mechanisms, rather than each country or company setting its own rules alone. Speeches like this often shape the direction of later regulation and international agreements, even though they don't create any binding rules by themselves.
