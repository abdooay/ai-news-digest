## What changed

On July 27, 2026, Anthropic published a statement from CEO Dario Amodei responding to accusations that the company has been lobbying to ban "open-weights" AI models. An open-weights model is one whose underlying parameters (the numbers that make the model work) are published for anyone to download and run, unlike a closed model that you can only access through a paid API.

The backdrop: reports had suggested US officials might restrict access to open-weights models made in China, and some tech companies signed a letter opposing such a move. Some critics claimed Anthropic wanted a ban to protect its own business. Amodei directly denies this: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models."

Instead, Amodei says Anthropic draws a line between open models that don't have dangerous capabilities, which he says provide real public value, and models with genuinely dangerous capabilities, regardless of whether they're open or closed. He lists three things Anthropic actually wants:

1. Restrictions on selling advanced computer chips to China, to limit how many powerful models China can build.
2. A crackdown on large-scale "distillation" operations — a technique where one AI model is used to train another, cheaper model to copy its abilities — when used to rapidly copy the capabilities of leading models.
3. Mandatory safety testing before release for any sufficiently capable model, open or closed, covering cybersecurity, biological, and alignment (whether the model behaves as intended) risks.

Amodei singled out biological risk as a particular concern, saying AI could eventually help someone recreate a dangerous, pandemic-level virus, and that this kind of risk tends to favor attackers over defenders.

## Why it matters

This is a policy clarification, not a product change, but it matters for anyone tracking AI regulation debates. Anthropic is trying to separate two different questions that often get merged in public debate: "should open models exist at all" (Anthropic says yes, mostly) versus "should specific dangerous capabilities be tested and restricted regardless of open or closed" (Anthropic says yes, this should be mandatory). If you build on open-weights models, this signals Anthropic's lobbying is aimed at chip exports and mandatory safety testing, not at restricting your ability to use or release open models generally.

## How to use it

There's nothing to install or configure. If you follow AI policy, watch for how "mandatory pre-release safety testing" proposals develop, since that's the concrete regulatory ask here, and it would apply to open and closed models alike.
